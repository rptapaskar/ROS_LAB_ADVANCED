00 · THE MISSION

Welcome to the
open-ended lab.

Experiments 1–6 taught you how the robot, ROS 2, sensors, motion and reactive control work. Experiment 07 asks a harder question: how accurately can the platform estimate its own motion, and how does that error grow? There is no single magic command that answers it.

🧠

You design the campaign

Prepare a one-page experimental plan with a hypothesis, measurements, trial count, controlled variables and metric.

📏

You need an independent reference

Odometry cannot validate itself. Compare reported position and heading against an independent physical reference.

📈

You fit a quantitative model

Use recorded data to estimate translational and rotational error coefficients, with dispersion and confidence intervals.

🔍

You explain the mechanism

Identify the dominant error source for the platform and surface, then explain why calibration cannot eliminate error growth.

The mindset shift: earlier labs asked “Can you implement this?” This lab asks “Can you design evidence strong enough to support a conclusion?”

02 · CAMPAIGN ARCHITECTURE

Three geometries.
One question.

The specified campaign uses straight-line, square and circular trajectories at repeatable lengths, on more than one surface and in both rotational directions. Each geometry reveals a different aspect of odometry error.

01

📏 Straight-line

Best for isolating distance-proportional translational error.

  • Repeat controlled lengths
  • Measure endpoint position
  • Compare reported vs physical distance
  • Repeat trials
02

⬛ Square

Combines translation and repeated rotations.

  • Use repeatable side length
  • Record final closure
  • Track heading drift
  • Compare rotational directions
03

⭕ Circular

Provides sustained rotational motion and repeated accumulation.

  • Repeat motion campaign
  • Measure pose against reference
  • Analyse error vs rotation
  • Support model fitting
Why multiple geometries? A single trajectory can hide an error mechanism. Straight-line motion emphasises translation; square and circular motion deliberately introduce rotation so the two effects can be separated.
03 · MEASUREMENT

Odometry needs
a referee.

An odometry estimate is not an independent measurement of itself. Your physical reference must be sufficiently independent and repeatable to make the comparison meaningful.

🤖

Reported pose

Record the robot's odometry-reported position and heading at the defined measurement points or trajectory endpoint.

📏

Physical reference

Use the independent physical reference method defined/approved for your laboratory. Measure position and heading with adequate repeatability.

🧭

Heading error

Compare the reported orientation with the physically measured orientation using a consistent angular convention.

📐

Position error

Compare estimated and physically measured position. State clearly whether you report component-wise error or a position-error magnitude.

📏

Position error

ep = ‖podom − pref‖

A useful scalar magnitude; document the exact metric selected by your group.

🧭

Heading error

eθ = wrap(θodom − θref)

Angular differences should be wrapped consistently so equivalent headings are not treated as huge errors.

Measurement quality is part of the experiment. If your physical reference is noisy or poorly defined, the fitted odometry coefficients can inherit that uncertainty.
04 · THE TWO-TERM IDEA

Separate error by
what causes growth.

The experiment asks you to fit a two-term error model separating distance-proportional from rotation-proportional error. The purpose is to determine whether error growth is better explained by travel distance, accumulated rotation, or both.

📏

Translational term

An error contribution that grows with travelled distance. It is useful for investigating wheel-radius mismatch, encoder scale effects and other mechanisms that accumulate as the robot travels.

e ≈ kd · D
🔄

Rotational term

An error contribution associated with accumulated rotation. It is useful for investigating track-width/turning-scale effects and other mechanisms revealed by repeated turning.

e ≈ kθ · Θ
🧩

Two-term model

e ≈ kdD + kθΘ

Your fitted model should state the units and definition of D, Θ and the error metric used.

⚠️

Model ≠ proof

fit → residuals → uncertainty → conclusion

A coefficient by itself does not prove causation. Examine dispersion, confidence intervals and residual behaviour.

06 · ERROR PROPAGATION

Systematic error
doesn't stay small.

The pre-lab explicitly asks you to review error propagation in dead reckoning and distinguish systematic from random error. This distinction is central to interpreting your repeated trials.

01

Systematic

Consistent directional bias or scale error that can accumulate predictably.

02

Random

Trial-to-trial variability that contributes to dispersion rather than a consistent drift direction.

03

Accumulation

Small systematic errors can become visible over longer distances or repeated turns.

04

Calibration limit

Calibration can reduce a known bias, but it cannot guarantee zero future error growth under changing conditions.

Think experimentally: if every trial drifts similarly, suspect a systematic mechanism. If trials scatter around a mean with no stable direction, random effects may dominate. Often both exist.
07 · FITTING THE MODEL

Now turn measurements
into coefficients.

The learning outcome requires a quantitative error model with coefficients, confidence intervals and residual analysis. Least-squares fitting is the pre-lab foundation for this stage.

📈 Least squares

Choose your response variable e and explanatory variables D and Θ according to the experimental design. Build a model matrix and estimate coefficients from the recorded observations.

e ≈ kdD + kθΘ

The fitted values should be reported with appropriate units and dispersion/uncertainty.

🎯 Confidence intervals

A coefficient estimate is not complete without an indication of uncertainty. Report confidence intervals using the statistical method appropriate to your fitted model and sample size.

estimate ± uncertainty

Interpret overlap with zero carefully: it can indicate that the data do not strongly distinguish that coefficient from zero at the selected confidence level.

Residual intuition

A good fit should be interrogated, not admired. Switch the illustrative dataset to see how residual structure changes.

Residuals fluctuate around zero without an obvious trend — a useful qualitative sign, but not sufficient by itself to validate a model.
08 · WHAT THE RESIDUALS SAY

Look for the thing
your model missed.

〰️

Random-looking residuals

If residuals appear patternless around zero, the selected model may be capturing the dominant trend reasonably well.

📈

Curved residuals

A systematic curve can indicate that the chosen two-term relationship is missing a nonlinear effect or another explanatory variable.

↗️

Residual trend

A persistent positive/negative trend suggests the model is leaving systematic structure unexplained.

🌪️

Changing spread

If residual spread grows with distance or rotation, uncertainty may not be constant across the operating range.

Engineering conclusion: do not select the mechanism because its story sounds plausible. Select the conclusion because the campaign, fitted coefficients, uncertainty and residual evidence support it.
09 · SURFACE + DIRECTION

Change the conditions.
See if the error changes.

The campaign is deliberately repeated on more than one surface and in both rotational directions. These are not extra data points for decoration — they are designed to expose systematic mechanisms.

🧱 Surface A

Repeat the controlled campaign on the first selected/assigned surface.

Control: keep trajectory and measurement method consistent.

🧱 Surface B

Repeat on a second surface so the platform's behaviour can be compared under changed contact conditions.

Ask: did the fitted error characteristics change?

↔️ Direction

Compare clockwise and counter-clockwise circuits/turning directions.

Direction dependence is evidence about systematic asymmetry.
Interpretation: if error reverses or changes substantially with rotational direction, that is a clue. If it remains similar, that is also evidence. Either way, quantify it.
10 · WHY CALIBRATION CANNOT “FIX EVERYTHING”

Calibrate the bias.
Then the robot moves again.

The final learning outcome asks you to explain why calibration cannot eliminate error growth. A calibration can correct an estimated systematic parameter under the conditions used to estimate it. But real motion still involves model mismatch, contact effects, encoder limitations, changing surfaces and measurement uncertainty.

🎯

Calibration can help

Correcting a known wheel-radius or track-width scale error can reduce a systematic component of odometry drift.

🌍

The world changes

Surface conditions, wheel contact, load and motion conditions can change the effective behaviour after calibration.

🧮

The model is incomplete

A differential-drive model is an abstraction. Unmodelled slip and mechanical effects can remain.

📏

Measurement has uncertainty

Your physical reference and odometry both contain measurement limitations. Report dispersion instead of pretending the estimate is exact.

11 · COMMON EXPERIMENTAL TRAPS

Don't accidentally
prove yourself right.

📐 Changing too many variables

Speed, surface, trajectory and direction all change at once.

You cannot tell which change caused the observed difference.

🔁 Too few trials

One impressive trajectory becomes the entire conclusion.

You cannot characterise dispersion or distinguish repeatability from luck.

🪞 Self-validation

Odometry is compared only with another software estimate.

The evidence is not sufficiently independent.

📈 Fit without residuals

A coefficient is reported because R² looks good.

A visually good fit can still hide structured residual error.

🎯 Overclaiming causation

A coefficient is interpreted as proof of a specific mechanical fault.

Association is not enough; the experimental design must support the mechanism claim.

🧹 Cherry-picking

Failed or inconvenient trials disappear from the dataset.

Document exclusions and reasons; preserve the integrity of the campaign.
