00 · THE MISSION

Fusion is not
magic.

Experiment 09 asks a deceptively simple question: does combining imperfect sensors produce a better pose estimate? You will compare encoder-only, IMU-only and fused orientation estimates against an independent reference, sweep a fusion parameter rather than assuming it, and evaluate short-term and long-term behaviour.

🛞

Encoder-only

Wheel/encoder information provides motion evidence, but accumulated dead-reckoning error can grow with travel and slip.

🧭

IMU-only

Inertial information provides orientation-related evidence, but its own bias and noise characteristics matter.

🧩

Fused estimate

Fusion combines complementary information using a weighting/filtering mechanism. Your experiment determines how the weighting affects the result.

🎯

Independent reference

The fused result must be evaluated against an independent physical reference rather than judged by whether the software looks smooth.

The key mindset: fusion is useful because sensors have complementary imperfections. But fusion does not create information that none of the sensors can observe.
01 · SENSOR FUSION

From imperfect signals
to a pose estimate.

The experiment introduces the principle of combining encoder and inertial information. Think of the sensors as two imperfect witnesses describing the same motion from different perspectives.

SOURCE 01

🛞 Encoders

Proprioceptive wheel-motion evidence.

  • Strong connection to travelled wheel motion
  • Useful for translational motion estimation
  • Can accumulate systematic error
  • Slip can break the assumed wheel-ground relationship
SOURCE 02

🧭 IMU

Inertial orientation/motion evidence.

  • Provides complementary inertial information
  • Useful for orientation estimation
  • Contains noise and bias
  • Long-term behaviour depends on sensor characteristics and fusion design
OUTPUT

🧩 Fusion

A weighted estimate produced from complementary information.

  • Combines sensor evidence
  • Weighting matters
  • Can improve some error modes
  • Cannot eliminate unobservable position drift by itself

THE FILTER IDEA

Extended Kalman Filter — concept, not black box.

The pre-lab asks you to review complementary filtering and the structure of the discrete extended Kalman filter. At a conceptual level, the filter predicts a state from a motion model and then uses measurements to correct that prediction.

STATE
pose / motion
PREDICT
motion model
MEASURE
sensor evidence
UPDATE
weighted correction
FUSED STATE
new estimate
Important: the filter's weighting is not a mystical “accuracy slider”. It represents how the fusion system balances competing information and uncertainty. This is why Experiment 09 requires an experimental parameter sweep.
02 · EXPERIMENTAL CAMPAIGN

Four tests.
One investigation.

The allocated laboratory sessions specify static-hold and single-turn tests first, followed by cumulative-rotation and induced-slip tests. Together they probe short-term stability, rotational behaviour and what happens when the assumptions behind dead reckoning are violated.

01

⏸️ Static hold

Keep the robot stationary and observe estimator behaviour over time.

  • Look for bias/drift
  • Compare estimator noise
  • Observe orientation stability
  • Use the reference consistently
02

↪️ Single turn

Perform a controlled single-turn test and compare orientation estimates.

  • Compare encoder-only
  • Compare IMU-only
  • Compare fused orientation
  • Measure error against reference
03

🔄 Cumulative rotation

Repeat rotational motion to expose long-term error accumulation.

  • Track error growth
  • Compare short vs long term
  • Inspect estimator stability
  • Report dispersion
04

🛞 Induced slip

Introduce controlled slip as specified by the laboratory procedure.

  • Observe assumption violation
  • Compare all estimators
  • Identify what fusion can/cannot correct
  • Defend the conclusion
05

🎯 Reference check

Every estimator needs comparison with the selected independent reference.

  • Position
  • Heading
  • Bias
  • Dispersion
06

⚖️ Parameter sweep

Do not assume one fusion weighting is automatically optimal.

  • Select a justified sweep
  • Run comparable trials
  • Compare error metrics
  • Identify the useful region
Design principle: keep the comparison fair. If the motion, reference, trial procedure or measurement convention changes between estimators, you are no longer comparing the estimators cleanly.
03 · THE DATA STORY

Measure more than
“looks good.”

Bias

Systematic tendency

Does an estimator consistently sit above or below the reference?

Noise

Short-term variation

How much does the estimate fluctuate during a controlled condition?

Drift

Long-term growth

Does error accumulate with time, rotation or motion?

Error

Reference difference

How far is the estimate from the independent reference?

Short-term accuracy

Static hold and single-turn tests help expose immediate estimator behaviour and orientation error without requiring a long trajectory.

e(t) = estimate(t) − reference(t)

Long-term accuracy

Cumulative rotation and induced-slip tests expose error mechanisms that may be small initially but become significant as motion accumulates.

error growth → evidence about the estimator
Report dispersion. A single mean error can hide a noisy estimator. Conversely, a low-noise estimator can still have a systematic bias. Your results table should preserve both aspects.

05 · WHY FUSION CAN HELP

Complementary errors
can be useful.

🛞 Encoder drift

Wheel-based dead reckoning can accumulate error as motion is integrated over time.

Fusion can use complementary inertial information to improve orientation estimation.

🧭 IMU bias/noise

Inertial measurements are not perfect and can contain bias and noise.

Fusion can constrain the estimate using other motion evidence.

🧩 Weighted compromise

The fused estimate depends on how information and uncertainty are represented in the filter.

That is why the weighting must be investigated experimentally.

⏱️ Short vs long term

An estimator that looks excellent over a short interval may behave differently over repeated motion.

Use both short-term and cumulative tests.

🛞 Slip

Slip violates the simple relationship between wheel rotation and actual body motion.

Ask whether the available sensors contain enough information to correct the violated assumption.

🎯 Reference

Fusion quality must be evaluated against an independent physical reference.

Smoothness is not accuracy.

08 · WHAT FUSION CANNOT ACHIEVE

This is the part
people miss.

The post-lab explicitly asks students to state why fusion of proprioceptive sensors bounds orientation error but not position error. The distinction is fundamental.

🧭 Orientation

Combining complementary orientation-related evidence can constrain heading/orientation error more effectively than relying on one imperfect source alone.

📍 Position

If all available information is still fundamentally based on integrated proprioceptive motion, position error can remain unbounded through accumulated model mismatch and slip.

🌍 Missing external reference

To correct global position drift, the system needs information that actually observes position relative to the world — not merely another sensor that integrates the same motion.

The key idea: sensor fusion can reduce uncertainty when sensors provide complementary information. It cannot manufacture an absolute position reference from purely proprioceptive measurements.
09 · SLIP: THE ASSUMPTION BREAKER

What happens when
the wheels lie?

Induced-slip tests are included specifically to challenge the motion assumptions behind encoder-based odometry. The interesting question is not simply whether error increases — it is whether the fusion architecture has enough independent information to recognise and compensate for the violated assumption.

🛞

Encoder story

The encoder can report wheel rotation even when the wheel-ground relationship does not produce the expected body motion.

🧭

IMU story

The inertial measurement provides a different source of motion/orientation evidence, but it also has its own imperfections.

🧩

Fusion story

The fused estimate can benefit from complementary evidence, but its success depends on what the available measurements actually observe.

🎯

Experimental story

Compare all estimators against the same reference. Quantify what changed under induced slip instead of relying on visual intuition.

10 · COMMON TRAPS

Don't let a smooth
line fool you.

🎨 Smooth ≠ accurate

A smooth fused curve can still have systematic error relative to the physical reference.

Always compare with the reference.

⚖️ One weighting

Choosing a fusion parameter because it “looks right” is not experimental evidence.

Sweep it and measure.

🛞 Ignore slip

Encoder odometry can fail when wheel rotation no longer represents body motion.

The slip test exists for this reason.

⏱️ Only short tests

A good single-turn result does not guarantee good long-term cumulative behaviour.

Use cumulative rotation.

🌳 Ignore TF

A correct numerical estimate attached to the wrong frame is still the wrong interpretation.

Verify the TF tree.

📍 Expect magic position

Combining proprioceptive sensors does not automatically create global position observability.

State the limitation explicitly.

12 · POST-LAB

Make the conclusion
conditional.

✓Comparative results: encoder-only, IMU-only and fused estimators compared across all tests.
✓Dispersion: variability reported, not just a single average.
✓Parameter sweep: fusion weighting investigated experimentally rather than assumed.
✓TF verification: transform tree checked against the REP-105 convention used by the experiment.
✓Error closure: report improvement or deterioration relative to raw wheel odometry.
✓Limitation: explain why proprioceptive fusion can constrain orientation error but does not by itself bound position error.
Strong conclusion structure: “Under test condition X, estimator A produced lower error than B by metric Y; the result changed/held under condition Z; the parameter sweep indicates …; therefore fusion is beneficial under …, but cannot achieve … because ….”
